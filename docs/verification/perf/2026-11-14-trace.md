# M3-1 performance trace (2026-11-14)

Roadmap M3-1: confirm the causes of TBT and LCP on the product page (pixel versus scene versus
framework) and confirm or replace the F-02 hypotheses of `docs/master-plan/audit.md`.

## Summary

- **The scene does not cause the LCP.** In every trace (live and production-config, 18 loads) the
  scene chunks are requested 12 to 33 s after navigation start, after the window `load` event
  (8 to 21 s) and well after the LCP (5 to 11 s). Aborting the scene chunks changes nothing outside
  the noise. F-02's "the scene mounts inside the Lighthouse window" is replaced for LCP and only
  partly holds for TBT.
- **The `794-*` chunk is Next's client runtime, not the scene.** The scene is `b536a0f1` plus
  `bd904a5c` (three, 83 plus 49 KB transferred) and `c15bf2b0` plus `50.*` (gsap and ScrollTrigger).
- **The measurement environment explains much of F-02's numbers.** The audit run had Lighthouse
  `benchmarkIndex` 631 (4x CPU slowdown on a loaded machine); the CI runners score about 2400. The
  CI lane (preset desktop, `cpuSlowdownMultiplier` 1, 10 Mbps) is not a mobile throttling profile,
  so its 100 scores and the audit's 31 are not comparable. This trace also ran at load average
  28 to 35 on 4 CPUs, so absolute times here are inflated too.
- Pixel, scene and framework all burn main-thread time, but only the framework runs before the LCP.
  Pixel and scene are post-load costs: they affect TBT/TTI and JS bytes, not the LCP.

## Method

- Playwright 1.64 isolated Chromium, Moto G Power-like viewport 412x823 at 2.625 DPR, mobile UA,
  CDP `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate` 4, `Network.emulateNetworkConditions` 150 ms RTT and
  1.6 Mbps down / 750 kbps up, service workers blocked, 15 s settle after `load`.
- CDP tracing via `browser.startTracing` (devtools.timeline, v8.execute, loading, v8 CPU profiler),
  plus in-page observers for LCP, longtask and long-animation-frame (LoAF). Attribution by script
  URL to a source bucket (framework, scene, gallery, pixel, Hotmart, other); chunk ids were mapped by
  grepping each chunk for markers (WebGLRenderer, ScrollTrigger, embla, react-dom, base-ui).
- 3 loads per configuration, medians. Trace JSON is not committed (tens of MB each); numbers are
  summarized below. The harness lived outside the repository and is not committed.
- **Live** `https://pequeverso.com/grafismo-fonetico/`, read-only, 9 loads in total (3 first
  attempt without the profiler, 3 with it, 3 with the scene chunks aborted). Route-aborted:
  `*.facebook.com`, `connect.facebook.net`, `pequeverso.com/api/*` (the PageView relay request was
  attempted and aborted every time). The buy CTA was never touched. Live serves commit `cf61f20`
  built 2026-10-19 (`/build-info.json`), so it predates the M0 to M3 work on `develop`; do not read
  it as the current code.
- **Production-config build** `npm run build:lhci` at `ed68f83` (fake pixel id, real third-party
  hosts), served from `out-lhci/` on a free port. Configurations: pixel (only `*.facebook.com`
  blocked, `fbevents.js` loads from `connect.facebook.net`, `/tr` blocked), no pixel
  (`connect.facebook.net` also blocked), pixel with the scene chunks aborted, and
  `/grafismo-fonetico/gracias/` as a control (no scene, no gallery).
- Cross-check: CI Lighthouse artifact of run 43592256267 (commit `7c69e40`, 3 runs per route).
- Chunk ids in this build (`0cc4fa0b`, `342d41ca`, `971-`) differ from the CI run's and live's
  (`4bd1b696`, `b536a0f1`, `794-`); the modules are the same, only the split changed.

## Caveats

- Load average 28 to 35 on 4 CPUs: run-to-run LCP spreads of 2x to 3x (for example no-scene live
  6.6 / 10.9 / 7.2 s). Treat differences below about 2 s as noise. The ordering of sources is more
  reliable than absolute values.
- The CPU profile samples (`native/program` is 60 to 70% of samples: parse, style, layout, paint,
  image decode, all inflated by the load) could not split pre- and post-LCP script time: my
  timestamp alignment put all JS after the LCP, which contradicts LoAF (scripts ran in frames
  before the LCP). Pre-LCP claims below rest on resource timings and LoAF, not on the profile.
- No Lighthouse run with mobile throttling was done on the production-config build (machine too
  loaded for a reliable simulated run); recommended as the first step of M3-2.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Live, scene aborted | 343 to 403 ms | 624 to 1062 ms | 2.6 to 2.7 s | 3.0 to 6.7 s | 7.2 s | 6.7 s |
| Prod-config, pixel | 35 to 368 ms | 575 to 1518 ms | 2.2 to 2.7 s | 0.7 to 2.6 s | 5.2 s | 4.7 s |

| Control `/gracias/` (pixel) | 26 to 104 ms | 640 to 1304 ms | 1.7 s | 1.4 to 1.8 s | 4.7 s | 4.0 s |

- Load time is a fixed 2.4 to 2.7 s: the hero AVIF over the emulated 1.6 Mbps link. Load delay
  (0.6 to 2.2 s) is the image waiting for the HTML parse and the CSS; render delay is main-thread
  contention and is the noisy part (hydration, style and layout while the box is loaded).
- Removing the scene or the pixel does not lower the LCP (the no-scene runs were slower, which is
  noise). Neither starts before the LCP: `fbevents.js` starts at 13 to 30 s and the scene chunks at
  12 to 33 s in every run.

## Cross-check with CI Lighthouse (run 43592256267, cpuSlowdown 1, 10 Mbps)

Product page, 3 runs: performance 100, LCP 909 to 1060 ms (median 928), TBT 18 to 66 ms (median
18). Boot time: `bd904a5c` (three) 275 ms scripting in the worst run, `794-*` about 150 ms,
`c15bf2b0` (gsap) 35 ms; Facebook 42 ms main thread (100 KB). Home: 100, LCP 860 ms, TBT 0.
Long tasks on the product page are 50 to 141 ms, headed by the scene chunks at about 1.6 to 1.7 s
(after the LCP): even unthrottled the scene is the largest single long task, but it is post-LCP.

## F-02 hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Live product page is 31 (LCP 10.1 s, TBT 6.6 s) | Unclear, likely inflated | The order of magnitude (LCP 7 to 9 s) reproduced only on a loaded machine with 4x throttle; audit `benchmarkIndex` 631 vs 2400 in CI; no mobile-throttled run on a quiet runner exists. |
| Chunks `b536a0f1` (85 KB) and `794-*` (68 KB, 3.1 s) are "scene and framework" | Replaced for `794-*` | `794-*` is Next's client runtime (router, scheduler); only `b536a0f1` (plus `bd904a5c` and the gsap chunks) is the scene. |
| The deferred scene mounts inside the Lighthouse window | Confirmed for TBT/TTI, rejected for LCP | Scene requests start after `load` (12 to 33 s) in every load; LCP is 5 to 11 s. Aborting the scene does not move the LCP. |
| The Meta Pixel adds to the saturation | Confirmed for TBT/TTI | 1.4 s self, about 100 KB, after `load`; not before the LCP. Not isolable in the totals because of the noise. |
| Pixel plus scene saturate a 4x CPU | Partly confirmed | Together about 3.2 s self after `load`; the framework (about 5 s) is the other part and gating does not touch it. |
| LCP cause (implicit) | Replaced | Hero AVIF load time (2.4 to 2.7 s at 1.6 Mbps) plus hydration contention in render delay; not the scene or the pixel. |

## Recommended order

1. **Fix the measurement first (M3-2 prerequisite).** Add a Lighthouse lane or settings with real
   mobile throttling (`cpuSlowdownMultiplier` 4, RTT 150, 1.6 Mbps) on a quiet runner, keep the 1x
   lane as the regression gate, and record the benchmark index. Without it the M3 before/after
   numbers are not comparable with F-02.
2. **M3-2 scene gating.** Desktop only (fine pointer plus min-width) removes the scene on mobile:
   155 to 250 KB of JS and about 1.8 s of self time at 4x (about 0.45 s unthrottled; the largest
   long task in CI, 100 to 140 ms). Expected effect: TBT and TTI, not LCP. Do not promise an LCP
   win from it.
3. **M3-3 JS budget.**
   a. Embla removal (chunk `971-`, with the lucide imports it pulls in): about 0.7 to 0.9 s self
      at 4x, in the initial scripts, the most LCP-relevant of the three.
   b. Pixel deferral to idle or first interaction (the consent default is a separate owner
      decision, F-01): about 1.4 s self and about 100 KB; it already runs after `load`, so the win
      is TBT/TTI only.
   c. Lazy zoom and Base UI are already out of the initial scripts; verify only that `203.*` stays
      out of the product page entry (no work expected).
4. The remaining LCP levers are the image (a smaller hero AVIF or a lower mobile `sizes` budget;
   load time 2.4 to 2.7 s at 1.6 Mbps is the floor) and fewer render-blocking CSS bytes before it
   (load delay 0.6 to 2.2 s).
